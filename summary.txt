World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia and Ukraine ended two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi with a deal for a large prisoner exchange — US envoy Jared Kushner called it the biggest humanitarian release of the war, with 1,000 to 1,500 per side discussed and numbers due Thursday — but no ceasefire or breakthrough on territory (BBC).

World leaders opened the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, where UN chief Antonio Guterres warned the climate crisis is outrunning us and urged a tax on fossil fuels; the US stayed away for a second year (AFP).

The US Justice Department charged three Iranian-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots in Europe and the US, including one at RAF Fairford, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after death threats (New York Times).

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said oil tankers caught fire after explosions in the Strait of Hormuz, and the UAE's ADNOC said one vessel was damaged in Emirati waters, renewing fears for shipping.

British counterterrorism police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer serving with a NATO unit in England on suspicion of spying for Russia.

Israel's new parliament was sworn in after a deadlocked election left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu without a majority and no clear path to a government.

The US House voted 211-204 to send eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively ending the effort in this Congress.

Millions of workers joined Spain's first general strike in 14 years over housing costs.

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% forecast, lifting the odds of a December Federal Reserve rate rise.

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to being declared Ebola-free.

Malaysia's former prime minister Najib Razak left prison for house arrest after four years over the 1MDB scandal.

Russia's ambassador to the US, Alexander Darchiev, died suddenly at 66.
